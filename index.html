~/about

AI engineering, built on a security foundation

I build intelligent systems that combine AI, automation, and secure technology to solve practical problems. Most of my work is AI engineering: designing AI agents and assistants, grounding them in real business knowledge so they answer accurately, and wiring them into the workflows and integrations that actually move a company's day forward.

I don't approach AI theoretically. I approach it as an engineer — what does the system need to do, where does the data come from, what happens when the model is unavailable, and who is allowed to see what. That instinct comes from my Bachelor in Information Technology & Computing — Networking and Security at the Arab Open University, and from my cooperative training inside Worley's IT department, where I worked with Active Directory, Windows deployment, ServiceNow, and live LAN infrastructure. Identity, access, networks, and safe data handling are not abstractions to me.

That combination is the point. Automation that touches customer data needs someone who thinks about security. AI that runs inside a business needs someone who understands the systems it plugs into. I work in Arabic and English, which is why the assistants I build are bilingual by default — including the one on this page.

Ali's Assistant — Bilingual AI Assistant

Live on this page

The assistant in the corner of this site. I designed and built it end-to-end: a bilingual (Arabic / English) assistant that answers questions about my background, with a hybrid architecture that never breaks — a grounded local intent engine that always works, plus an optional LLM path for freeform questions.

Problem
A visitor with one specific question shouldn't have to read an entire portfolio to find the answer.
My role
Sole designer and developer — architecture, knowledge base, Arabic language handling, UI.
AI
Optional LLM path with a grounded system instruction that restricts answers to verified facts and refuses to invent; falls back silently to the local engine on any API failure or rate limit.
Engineering
Arabic text normalization (diacritics, tatweel, أ/إ/آ and ة/ه variants), weighted intent matching, Gulf-dialect keywords, context memory, and transcript persistence.
Security
Assistant output is treated as untrusted: rendered as DOM text nodes, never HTML, so only recognized links become anchors.
